# Far field plots and 3D pattern

These tabs show where the antenna sends its power. Both need a calculation: press **Calculate**
on the tab.

## Far field plots

The left plot is the **horizontal pattern**, seen from above at the elevation of the strongest
radiation. 0° is +X, the antenna's front, and angles go anticlockwise towards +Y at 90°. The right
plot is the **vertical pattern**, seen from the side in the direction of the strongest radiation,
with the front on the right.

The rings are 0, −3, −6, −10, −20 and −30 dB below the maximum, on a logarithmic scale.

**Show** picks the polarization: **Total**, **Horizontal** or **Vertical**, or any mix of them.

**Click a plot** to move the orange gain marker there. The gain in that direction is shown in the
corner, in dBi and in dB below the maximum. On the vertical pattern this reads the gain at a
low elevation, for example. The lines under each plot repeat the main results.

## 3D pattern

The 3D pattern shows the gain in every direction as a solid. Its colour runs from blue (weak) to
red (strong).

- **Drag** to rotate, **right-drag** to move it, and use the **wheel** to zoom.
- **Double-click** or **Reset** returns to the starting view. **Top**, **Front** and **Side** are set views.
- **Hover** over the pattern to read the gain in that direction.
- **Grid lines** draws the mesh.
- **Scale** sets how the distance from the centre shows the gain:
  - **ARRL**: a log-periodic scale that shows the main lobe and small lobes together. It is the one most antenna books use.
  - **dB**: linear in decibels, down to the floor shown in the colour bar.
  - **Linear**: in proportion to the field strength. Small lobes almost vanish.

Over ground, the ground's surface is drawn as a disc.
